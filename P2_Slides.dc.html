Segment 2
Offensive vs defensive security
& the career landscape
Two ways of working on the same problem — and the jobs that come out of each.
Segment 2 · Concept 1 of 2
Offensive security
Working as an attacker, with permission, to find weaknesses in a system before a real adversary does. The tools and techniques are the adversary's; the authorisation, the scope and the reporting are what make it legitimate professional work.
Why it matters
A weakness nobody has tested is a weakness nobody has fixed. Offensive work turns theory about risk into evidence.
In this programme
The CyberOps specialisation. Thirteen weeks from reconnaissance and scanning through to a full attack chain in the capstone.
Segment 2 · Concept 2 of 2
Defensive security
Preventing attacks where possible, detecting them when prevention fails, and investigating what happened so the organisation can recover and improve. Defence is judged on how quickly and accurately an incident is understood, not only on whether an attack was stopped.
Why it matters
Attacks are continuous. An organisation cannot buy its way out of them, so it must be able to see, explain and contain them.
In this programme
The DFIR specialisation — digital forensics and incident response — from forensic methodology through to SOC operations and threat hunting.
Segment 2 · Teams
Red, blue and purple
Red team
Attacks
Simulates a real adversary against the organisation, within an agreed scope, to test whether defences actually work.
Blue team
Defends
Monitors, detects, investigates and responds. Owns the logs, the alerts and the incident process.
Purple team
Connects
Not a separate department. Red and blue working together so that every attack technique produces a detection improvement.

Career landscape · Role 1 of 4
SOC Analyst
Works in a security operations centre, monitoring alerts from across the organisation. Triages what matters, escalates what is serious, and closes what is benign — with a written reason every time.
The daily question
“Is this alert real, and how urgent is it?” Volume is the challenge; judgement is the skill.
Where this programme prepares you
DFIR track: SOC operations, monitoring, level two investigation, log analysis and detection engineering.
Career landscape · Role 2 of 4
Penetration Tester
Carries out authorised, ethically driven tests of an organisation's defences, using the same tools and techniques as a malicious actor. The work is defined by a scope, and it ends in a report the client can act on.
The daily question
“How would an attacker get in from here, and can I prove it?”
Where this programme prepares you
CyberOps track: reconnaissance, enumeration, web application vulnerabilities, exploitation, Active Directory, and reporting.
Career landscape · Role 3 of 4
DFIR Analyst
Digital forensics and incident response. Recovers and interprets evidence from disks, memory, logs and devices to establish what happened, then supports the response and the recovery. The findings must stand up to challenge.
The daily question
“What exactly happened, in what order, and what can I prove with evidence?”
Where this programme prepares you
DFIR track: forensic methodology, Windows and Linux artefacts, memory forensics, malware triage and the incident response process.
Career landscape · Role 4 of 4
Governance, Risk & Compliance
Sets and checks the rules. Maps an organisation's security posture against standards and regulation, assesses risk, and evidences compliance. Less time at a terminal, more time in policy, assessment and writing.
The daily question
“What are we required to do, are we doing it, and can we show that we are?”
Where this programme prepares you
F3 covers governance and regulation, the GRC framework, and relating a security posture to ISO 27001 and NIST 800-53.

Segment 3 · Skills
What employers actually test
Clear writing
Reports and incident notes are the product. If a manager cannot follow it, the work did not happen.
Spoken briefing
Explaining a finding out loud, in English, to someone non-technical and under time pressure.
Evidence discipline
Taking notes as you go, recording what you did and when. Both tracks are judged on it.
Time management
Knowing when to stop digging and escalate. Both a SOC shift and an engagement are time-boxed.
Teamwork & handover
Security runs in shifts and in teams. Your handover is another analyst's starting point.
Ethics & permission
Non-negotiable. Ethics, permission and trust are upheld through every engagement — the whole profession rests on it.
Segment 3 · Mindset
Simulate an attacker to find weaknesses before real adversaries do.
The shift
Stop asking “how is this supposed to work?” and start asking “how can this be misused?”
Why defenders need it
You cannot detect a technique you have never imagined. This is exactly why the two tracks meet for purple team exercises.

Common misconceptions
Get these right early
✗ “Offensive security means hacking without permission.”
✓ Authorisation and scope are what make it lawful professional work.
✗ “Purple team is a third team with its own staff.”
✓ It is red and blue working together — an activity, not a headcount.
✗ “Forensics and incident response are the same job.”
✓ Forensics establishes what happened; incident response decides and acts on what to do.
✗ “Only technical skill matters in cybersecurity.”
✓ Writing, briefing and evidence discipline decide who is trusted with real work.
