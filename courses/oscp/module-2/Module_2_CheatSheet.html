OSCP: Introduction to Cybersecurity

2.1 THE PRACTICE OF CYBERSECURITY
Adversarial: an intelligent opponent adapts to whatever you build
Asymmetric: defender must hold every path, attacker needs one
Mirror image: every technique implies a mitigation; every control implies a bypass
Mindsets: assume nothing · think in systems · evidence over belief · persist deliberately
2.3 THE CIA TRIAD
Confidentiality: disclosure only to the authorised — the leg most tests attack
Integrity: alteration only by the authorised — quieter failure, higher recovery cost
Availability: reachable when required — normally OUT of test scope
In tension: maximising one leg costs the others; the balance is a business decision
Use it to: phrase impact — name the leg, the asset, and who gains the capability
2.4 PRINCIPLES
Defence in depth: layers, each assumed to fail; beware shared failure modes
Least privilege: only what the task needs, only while needed
No secrecy of design: obscurity is a layer, never the layer
Threat intelligence: earns its cost only through decisions it changes
Policy: access & identity · change & configuration · incident & acceptable use
2.2 RISK · THREAT · VULNERABILITY · EXPLOIT — ONE SITUATION, FOUR STATEMENTS
TermWhat it isIn one situation
ThreatAn actor or circumstance able to cause harm; exists independently of youA criminal group monetising access to corporate networks
VulnerabilityA weakness in a system, configuration, process or personA file server whose service binary path is writable
ExploitThe mechanism that takes advantage of the weaknessReplacing the binary and restarting the service to gain SYSTEM
RiskLikelihood combined with impact, in this environmentHigh — the host stores the finance department's shared drive
Threat actor classes: opportunistic (automated breadth) · organised criminal (return on investment) · state-sponsored (patience, resourcing) · insider (pre-existing trust) · activist (cause) · authorised tester (bounded by scope)

2.5 LAW · REGULATION · STANDARD · FRAMEWORK   |   2.6 CAREER PATHS
InstrumentWhat it isIgnoring it means
LawLegislation enacted by a stateCriminal or civil liability
RegulationBinding rules issued under lawFines, enforcement, loss of licence
StandardAuditable requirements — certifiableFailed certification, lost contracts
FrameworkStructure for organising activity — adoptedNo penalty, no defensible structure
Offensive: penetration testing, red teaming, adversary simulation
Defensive: security operations, detection engineering, incident response, threat hunting
Advisory / engineering: security architecture, GRC, secure development
OSCP signals: hands-on capability under uncertainty + documentation. It does not signal client management, scoping or seniority.
EXAM TRAPS & ACRONYMS
⚠️ Risk ≠ vulnerability — a vulnerability is a weakness in the system; risk is likelihood × impact in context
⚠️ Threats exist independently of you and cannot be eliminated — defence works on vulnerabilities, impact and attacker cost
⚠️ No public exploit does not mean no vulnerability
⚠️ Availability is normally OUT of test scope — disruptive techniques need written agreement
⚠️ Obscurity is worthless as the only control, but legitimate as an extra layer above one that holds
⚠️ Standards certify; frameworks organise. You cannot be "certified" against a framework
⚠️ Authorisation, in writing and in advance, is the only thing separating this work from the offence it resembles
