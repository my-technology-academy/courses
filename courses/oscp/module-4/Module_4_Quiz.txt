
QUESTIONS:

Q1. Which section of a technical report is written specifically for a non-technical, decision-making reader?
    (A) The technical summary
    (B) The executive summary
    (C) The findings section
    (D) The appendices

Q2. Why does note portability matter over the course of a long engagement or a long study programme?
    (A) It reduces file size on disk
    (B) Notes must survive tool, machine and time changes without being locked into a proprietary format
    (C) It is a legal requirement in every jurisdiction
    (D) It allows notes to be automatically translated into other languages

Q3. What is the correct fixed anatomy for describing an individual technical finding?
    (A) Title, date, tester name, signature
    (B) Description, evidence, impact, recommendation
    (C) Summary, appendix, glossary, index
    (D) Scope, exclusions, constraints, timeline

Q4. Why should the executive summary generally be written after the technical findings are drafted?
    (A) Because summarising requires knowing what was actually found, not what was expected at the outset
    (B) Because clients only read reports in reverse order
    (C) Because it is a formatting requirement of most word processors
    (D) Because technical findings cannot be edited once the summary exists

Q5. What is the purpose of stating test environment considerations in a report?
    (A) To pad the report to a required page count
    (B) To prevent a reader from mistaking a scoped or constrained result for an unconditional one
    (C) To list the tester's personal equipment
    (D) To replace the need for an executive summary



ANSWER KEY (For Instructor Use)
==================================================
Q1: (B) The executive summary
Q2: (B) Notes must survive tool, machine and time changes without being locked into a proprietary format
Q3: (B) Description, evidence, impact, recommendation
Q4: (A) Because summarising requires knowing what was actually found, not what was expected at the outset
Q5: (B) To prevent a reader from mistaking a scoped or constrained result for an unconditional one
==================================================
