2.  Certification Overview
The UKAIF is a foundation-level certification that verifies an individual possesses a modern, practical understanding of artificial intelligence, generative AI and the responsible use of AI in the workplace. It is designed for a general professional audience rather than for engineers or specialists, and it assumes no prior AI knowledge.
UKAIF establishes a common baseline of AI literacy: what AI is and is not, how today’s generative AI and large-language-model systems work at an appropriate conceptual level, how to use and prompt these tools effectively, why their outputs must be verified, and how to handle AI responsibly with due regard for security, privacy, ethics and organisational accountability.
The certification is theory-based. Understanding is assessed through a structured multiple-choice examination. UKAIF is deliberately broad rather than deep: its purpose is to build confident, informed and responsible everyday users of AI, and to provide the shared foundation on which more specialised AI study can later be built.
Positioning. UKAIF plays the same role for artificial intelligence that a strong entry-level cybersecurity certification plays for security: a broad, credible foundation taken before progressing into more specialised practitioner and advanced pathways. It is not an advanced technical AI course, a programming course, an AI engineering qualification or an AI security specialist certification.
3.  Purpose of the Certification
UKAIF exists to address a simple, growing need: AI is now part of everyday work across virtually every profession, yet most people have never been given a structured, reliable grounding in how it works or how to use it well. The certification sets a clear, assessable standard for that grounding.
The purpose of UKAIF is to:
Provide a vendor-neutral foundation in modern AI, generative AI and large-language-model concepts.
Enable individuals to use common AI tools effectively, safely and responsibly in a workplace context.
Establish that a certified individual understands the limitations of AI and the need to verify its output.
Promote responsible AI use, sound data handling, privacy awareness and human accountability.
Serve as the recognised entry point into a wider AI certification pathway, before candidates specialise.
4.  Who the Certification Is For
UKAIF is intentionally broad in its applicability. AI literacy is increasingly relevant across almost every role, so the certification is written for a general professional and learner audience rather than for a single technical discipline.
Typical candidates include:
•  People completely new to AI•  Employees beginning to use AI at work•  Business professionals•  Managers and team leaders•  Students•  Career changers•  Cybersecurity professionals•  IT professionals•  Sales and marketing professionals•  HR professionals•  Operations teams•  Consultants•  Entrepreneurs and small-business owners•  Anyone preparing for more advanced AI study
UKAIF is not positioned as an IT-only qualification. It is designed to be equally valuable to a marketer, an HR manager, an operations lead or a student as it is to a technologist.
5.  Prerequisites
None. There are no prerequisites for UKAIF. No prior AI knowledge, technical background, programming ability or mathematics is required or assumed. The certification is designed to be accessible to a complete beginner while still being substantial enough to be worth holding.
6.  What Candidates Will Learn
Across the programme, candidates build a connected picture of modern AI — moving from core concepts, to how systems work, to practical and responsible everyday use. On completion, candidates will be able to:
Understand and correctly use the core vocabulary of modern AI, including machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, large language models and foundation models.
Explain, without mathematics, how AI systems learn and produce output — training and inference, datasets, parameters, tokens, context windows and embeddings.
Recognise the main categories of AI tools and generative AI, including multimodal AI, AI agents and retrieval-augmented generation, and select an appropriate tool for a task.
Write clear, effective prompts and apply AI to common workplace tasks such as drafting, summarising, planning and research assistance.
Identify the limitations and risks of AI — including hallucination and overconfidence — and apply verification and human oversight.
Protect privacy, confidential information and sensitive data, and recognise foundational AI security risks.
Apply the principles of responsible AI: fairness, transparency, disclosure, accountability, human decision-making and organisational policy.

9.  Detailed Examination Objectives
The objectives below define what is assessable within each domain. They are written at foundation level: the emphasis is on conceptual understanding and practical judgement, not technical implementation.
  DOMAIN 1  —  AI Concepts &amp; Terminology      (20%)  
Define artificial intelligence and distinguish it from ordinary automation and traditional rule-based software.
Explain how artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI relate to one another as nested, layered ideas.
Define and correctly use core terms: model, dataset, parameters, tokens, context window, foundation model, large language model (LLM), multimodal AI and embeddings.
Distinguish today’s task-specific (&quot;narrow&quot;) AI from the concept of general AI.
Distinguish generative AI from traditional predictive or analytical AI at a conceptual level.
Recognise common examples of AI already encountered in everyday life and at work.
  DOMAIN 2  —  How AI Systems Work      (15%)  
Explain, without mathematics, that modern AI produces output by prediction, and that a language model works by predicting the next piece of text.
Distinguish training (how a model is built from data) from inference (how a trained model is used).
Explain the conceptual role of datasets and parameters in shaping what a model can do.
Explain tokens and context windows, and why they create practical limits on length and memory.
Explain what a knowledge cut-off is and its consequences for currency of information.
Explain temperature / randomness and why identical prompts can produce different answers (non-determinism).
Understand, at a conceptual level, that APIs allow other applications to connect to and use AI models, and that embeddings are a way meaning is represented numerically.
  DOMAIN 3  —  Generative AI &amp; AI Tools      (15%)  
Describe the main categories of generative AI: text, image, audio, video and code generation.
Explain what a foundation model is and how a single general-purpose model can be applied to many tasks.
Identify the main categories of AI tools and platforms (vendor-neutral) and their typical workplace uses.
Explain, at a foundation level, what an AI agent is — a system that can take actions or use tools to complete a task.
Explain retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conceptually as a way of connecting a model to external or up-to-date information.
Understand multimodal AI — systems that work across more than one type of input or output.
Assess the strengths and limitations of AI tools well enough to select an appropriate tool for a given task.
  DOMAIN 4  —  Prompting &amp; Effective AI Use      (15%)  
Apply a structured approach to prompting using task, context, format and constraints.
Write clear, specific and effective instructions, and diagnose and improve weak prompts.
Use iterative refinement and follow-up prompting to improve results.
Provide examples, roles or personas to guide the style and content of AI output.
Apply AI effectively to common workplace tasks such as drafting, summarising, planning and research assistance.
Judge which tasks are appropriate for AI assistance and which are not.
  DOMAIN 5  —  AI Limitations &amp; Verification      (15%)  
Explain what hallucination is and why AI can produce confident but incorrect information.
Explain overconfidence and the fact that a model does not &quot;know&quot; when it is wrong.
Explain how knowledge cut-offs and missing or outdated information affect reliability.
Apply verification and source-checking techniques to AI output.
Explain the role of human-in-the-loop review and human oversight.
Identify tasks where verification is essential, such as facts, figures, and legal, medical, financial or code-related output.
  DOMAIN 6  —  AI Security, Privacy &amp; Data      (10%)  
Explain why personal, confidential, sensitive or regulated information should not be entered into unapproved AI tools.
Describe, at a foundation level, how inputs may be stored or used, and what is meant by data leakage.
Recognise &quot;shadow AI&quot; — the use of unsanctioned AI tools — and why it creates risk.
Show awareness of AI-enabled threats, including deepfakes, AI-assisted phishing and social engineering, and misinformation.
Show conceptual awareness of prompt injection and manipulated inputs as a category of risk.
Apply basic safe practices: use approved tools, protect account access and credentials, and follow data-classification and handling rules.
  DOMAIN 7  —  Responsible AI, Ethics &amp; Governance      (10%)  
Explain bias and fairness, and how bias can enter AI systems through data.
Explain the importance of transparency, disclosure and explainability in AI use.
Describe accountability — that humans remain responsible for decisions made with AI assistance.
Understand the role of organisational AI policy, acceptable-use rules and governance at a foundation level.
Recognise, at a generic awareness level, that AI regulation and standards are emerging and evolving.
Apply responsible-use principles and maintain human oversight in workplace decision-making.

11.  Assessment Methodology
UKAIF is assessed by a single multiple-choice certification examination. The examination is designed to test genuine understanding and applied judgement rather than rote memorisation: questions include both direct knowledge items and short scenario-based items that ask the candidate to choose the most appropriate action or interpretation.
Questions are drawn from a substantially larger internal question bank and assembled to match the published domain weightings, so that every examination provides balanced coverage across all seven domains while varying between candidates and attempts. The examination returns an immediate pass or fail result against a fixed pass mark.
Formative assessment. Before attempting the certification examination, candidates can check their readiness using the module quizzes, scenario-based knowledge questions and the dedicated practice examination questions, which mirror the style and domain balance of the real examination.

13.  Recommended Study Approach
UKAIF is designed for self-paced study and requires no prior knowledge. The following approach is recommended for most candidates:
Work through the eight modules in order, using the slides, module summaries and flash cards to build and consolidate understanding.
Use the AI tutor to explore any concept that is unclear, and try the ideas out with a common AI tool where possible to make them concrete.
After each module, complete the module quiz and the scenario-based knowledge questions to check understanding.
Use the cheat sheet for consolidated revision across all domains.
When ready, complete the practice examination questions under realistic conditions, and review any weak domains before booking the certification examination.
Because the examination is weighted, candidates should give proportionally more revision time to the higher-weighted domains — particularly Domain 1 (AI Concepts &amp; Terminology, 20%) — while ensuring every domain is covered.

15.  Certificate and Digital Badge
On passing the examination, the candidate is awarded a digital UKAIF certificate and a digital badge. The certificate records the holder’s name, the certification title (UKAIF) and the credential level. The digital badge can be displayed on professional profiles, email signatures and CVs to indicate that the holder has met the UKAIF standard of foundational AI literacy.
16.  Progression Pathway
UKAIF is the entry point into a wider AI certification pathway. It establishes the common foundation required before candidates move into deeper, more specialised study — whether in AI use, AI security, governance, technical or other specialist directions.

Appendix A — Curriculum Review &amp; Enhancement Notes (Internal)
For the content team, not for publication. This appendix records the review of the existing curriculum against the seven examination domains, and the foundation-level enhancements recommended. The seven-domain architecture and weightings are unchanged.
Recommended foundation-level additions (already reflected above)
Concept hierarchy (Domain 1): make the nesting of AI ⊃ machine learning ⊃ deep learning, with generative AI and LLMs as a subset, explicit so learners can place terms relative to each other. Add a light narrow-vs-general AI distinction.
Generative vs. predictive AI (Domain 3): add a clear conceptual contrast so candidates don’t treat &quot;AI&quot; and &quot;generative AI&quot; as synonyms.
New foundation concepts (Domains 1–3): foundation models, tokens, context windows, parameters, embeddings, multimodal AI, AI agents, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and APIs — all introduced conceptually only, folded into Modules 1–3 rather than as new modules.
Transparency &amp; explainability (Domain 7): added alongside disclosure and accountability.
Most important gap identified — AI security (Domain 6)
The single significant gap: the existing modules covered ethics and data privacy well, but had no explicit AI security content, while Domain 6 is titled &quot;AI Security, Privacy &amp; Data&quot; and carries 10% of the examination. To give this domain a credible source, the recommendation is to re-cast the final two modules as shown in Section 10:
Module 7 → &quot;AI Security, Privacy &amp; Data&quot; (feeds Domain 6): data leakage, not entering sensitive/confidential data into unapproved tools, shadow AI, AI-enabled threats such as deepfakes and AI-assisted phishing, and conceptual prompt-injection awareness — all at awareness level, not technical depth.
Module 8 → &quot;Ethics, Bias, Responsible AI &amp; Governance&quot; (feeds Domain 7): consolidates the previous ethics and responsible-AI content and adds governance and transparency.
This is a content re-organisation only — it keeps eight modules and does not alter any domain or weighting. It is flagged here (rather than changed silently) per the brief.
One item to confirm (no change made)
Domain balance: Domain 6 now carries both security and privacy at 10%. This is workable at foundation level, but if the security content proves substantial in authoring, you may later wish to review whether 10% still feels proportionate. No change has been made; raised only for awareness.
Scope discipline: all additions are conceptual. No Python, mathematics, model-building, technical labs or engineering content has been introduced, in line with the brief’s breadth-over-depth objective.
