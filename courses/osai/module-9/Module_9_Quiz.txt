Q1. Why is cloud identity treated as a cross-cutting, fifth component of the infrastructure layer rather than alongside the server, framework, and compute components?
    (A) Because identity is a piece of hardware just like a GPU
    (B) Because identity is the permission boundary that determines how far a compromise of any other component can spread
    (C) Because identity is only relevant to Objective 9.2, not the rest of the module
    (D) Because identity cannot be compromised through infrastructure at all

Q2. A serving deployment's primary inference API requires authentication, but a nearby metrics endpoint does not, because it was assumed to be reachable only from a trusted internal network. What does this scenario illustrate?
    (A) A denial-of-wallet finding, since cost was affected
    (B) A control assumed to exist at the network-position layer was never enforced at the interface itself
    (C) A dataset-poisoning finding, since the metrics endpoint returns training data
    (D) A cloud-identity privilege-escalation finding, since credentials were exposed

Q3. During an engagement, a tester finds that a serving process runs under a cloud identity with far more permission than inference alone requires. Why does this matter even if the tester cannot demonstrate a specific escalation chain?
    (A) It does not matter, since excess privilege alone is never a reportable finding
    (B) Because a future compromise of the serving process would inherit that excess privilege, extending the impact well beyond the model itself
    (C) Because OWASP LLM10 requires this exact finding to be reported
    (D) Because excess privilege always indicates a backdoored model is present

Q4. Why does denial of wallet not require an availability impact to be considered a complete, successful finding?
    (A) Because denial of wallet is not a real risk unless the service goes offline
    (B) Because it exploits a per-request cost asymmetry to inflate operating cost, which is itself the targeted property, independent of uptime
    (C) Because cost and availability are always affected together in AI infrastructure
    (D) Because OWASP LLM10 only applies when the service becomes unavailable

Q5. Why is cloud identity and privilege-escalation risk from AI infrastructure (Objective 9.3) reported without forcing it into a dedicated OWASP LLM Top 10 category?
    (A) Because it is not a real risk in AI red teaming engagements
    (B) Because privilege escalation from a compromised host is a general infrastructure-security concern that applies identically to non-AI workloads
    (C) Because OWASP LLM10 already fully covers this risk
    (D) Because MITRE ATLAS forbids infrastructure findings from being reported
