
QUESTIONS:

Q1. An attacker hides an instruction inside an internal document. When a user later asks a question, a RAG-enabled agent retrieves that document and follows the hidden instruction to email data to the attacker. Which term best describes how the payload was delivered?
    (A) Direct prompt injection
    (B) Indirect prompt injection
    (C) Jailbreaking
    (D) Model extraction

Q2. What most distinguishes AI red teaming from a penetration test?
    (A) It never exploits weaknesses, only enumerates and ranks them
    (B) It is objective-driven, emulating a real adversary and chaining weaknesses to prove a specific impact
    (C) It is limited to eliciting harmful content from the model
    (D) It measures success purely by how much of the attack surface is covered

Q3. In an OSAI report, which framework is used to name and classify what can go wrong in an LLM application?
    (A) MITRE ATLAS
    (B) NIST AI Risk Management Framework
    (C) OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications
    (D) The EU AI Act

Q4. How does jailbreaking differ from prompt injection?
    (A) Jailbreaking targets the model's content policy, whereas injection subverts the application's intended control flow
    (B) Jailbreaking requires physical access, whereas injection is always remote
    (C) Jailbreaking affects only predictive models, whereas injection affects only generative models
    (D) They are identical terms with no meaningful distinction

Q5. Why does the agent and tool layer typically carry the greatest impact in an AI system?
    (A) Because tool outputs are always encrypted and therefore trusted
    (B) Because autonomy and function-calling turn a language trick into real actions, such as querying databases or sending email
    (C) Because agents cannot be monitored by defenders
    (D) Because the model weights are exposed through every tool call
