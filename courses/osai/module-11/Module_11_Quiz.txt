
QUESTIONS:

Q1. During the capstone, a tester spots an interesting-looking system just outside the agreed scope boundary. What does Objective 11.1's scope discipline call for?
    (A) Pursue it immediately, since any demonstrated compromise strengthens the report
    (B) Note it and do not pursue it, since findings outside agreed boundaries are not a successful engagement outcome
    (C) Pursue it only if it can be exploited faster than in-scope components
    (D) Expand the scope unilaterally without clarifying with the engagement stakeholders

Q2. Why does Objective 11.2 warn that jumping straight to exploitation is the most common capstone and exam failure mode, even against a target that looks simple?
    (A) Because exploitation techniques from Modules 3 to 9 do not work without threat modeling
    (B) Because it feels productive in the moment but reliably underperforms a disciplined recon-then-threat-model opening that picks the highest-ranked path
    (C) Because reconnaissance is legally required before any testing begins
    (D) Because threat modeling always takes less time than exploitation

Q3. Midway through the engagement window, a tester has confirmed ten isolated weaknesses but has not chained any toward the scoped objective, and no report content has been written. What is the most defensible next action?
    (A) Continue enumerating additional isolated weaknesses, since more findings always strengthen the final report
    (B) Stop enumerating, select the highest-ranked candidate chain toward the scoped objective, execute it, and begin writing the report incrementally
    (C) Immediately write the final report using only the ten isolated findings, since reporting time is more important than chaining
    (D) Discard all ten findings and restart reconnaissance, since none of them has yet been chained into a path

Q4. Why does Objective 11.3 state that technique breadth alone does not outscore demonstrated impact?
    (A) Because a smaller number of findings chained to the scoped objective proves the client-relevant risk, while many unchained findings do not
    (B) Because OSAI only allows a maximum of three findings to be reported per engagement
    (C) Because technique breadth is never relevant to an engagement's outcome
    (D) Because chained findings always take less time to document than isolated ones

Q5. Why does Objective 11.4 recommend writing the report incrementally throughout the engagement rather than after exploitation is "finished"?
    (A) Because reports written incrementally are graded more leniently
    (B) Because a report built from real-time notes and the living kill-chain document is both faster to finish and more accurate than one reconstructed afterward, and learners consistently underestimate reporting time
    (C) Because the report must be submitted before exploitation begins
    (D) Because incremental writing removes the need for evidence per step
