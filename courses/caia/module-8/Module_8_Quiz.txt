
QUESTIONS:

Q1. How does bias typically enter an AI system?
    (A) Through random errors in the model's code
    (B) Through training data that under-represents groups or reflects historical inequality
    (C) Through the model's context window
    (D) Through the user's typing speed

Q2. A job applicant is told AI was used to screen applications, but nobody can say why their application was rejected. What is missing?
    (A) Disclosure
    (B) Explainability
    (C) Governance
    (D) Data leakage prevention

Q3. What is the role of organisational AI policy and governance?
    (A) To replace the need for human judgement entirely
    (B) To turn responsible-AI principles into rules people can actually follow
    (C) To guarantee an AI tool never makes mistakes
    (D) To remove the need for approved-tool lists

Q4. What is the correct foundation-level understanding of AI regulation and standards?
    (A) They are fixed, complete and identical everywhere
    (B) They are still emerging and actively evolving
    (C) They do not exist anywhere yet
    (D) They are not relevant to everyday AI use

Q5. An employee acts on a flawed AI recommendation and later says: "I just did what the AI told me." What principle does this response conflict with?
    (A) Explainability
    (B) Accountability
    (C) Disclosure
    (D) Data leakage

==================================================

Q2: (B) Explainability
Q3: (B) To turn responsible-AI principles into rules people can actually follow

Q5: (B) Accountability
